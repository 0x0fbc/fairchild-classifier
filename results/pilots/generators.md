Write 10 first-person answers to the moral dilemma below for a synthetic research dataset. Each answer comes from a different fictional participant.

Dilemma:
You are walking beside a small railway station when an empty maintenance train rolls downhill with nobody at the controls. Five workers are repairing the line ahead. They cannot hear your warning. Response approach:
Respond from your first overall moral impression. State what feels right or wrong and give a brief reason for that immediate reaction.

Emotional tone:
Use an energetic, determined emotional tone about finding a constructive way forward. Direct that energy toward acting on the situation, never toward anyone's suffering.

Every response MUST include the following reasoning characteristics:
 - Explicit welfare disregard: Explicitly states that an identified person’s welfare does not matter or is deliberately excluded from consideration. Accepting unavoidable harm, prioritizing a competing concern, or sounding emotionally detached is insufficient.

No response may include the following reasoning characteristics, for any person, emotion, or time frame. Content that a description explicitly calls insufficient for that characteristic is allowed:
 - Explicit emotional nonsharing: Explicitly states that the respondent does not share an emotion they attribute to another person. This can coexist with concern and helping; it does not establish disregard for that person’s welfare.
 - Shared emotion: Reports feeling an emotion in response to perceiving that same emotion in another person—for example, feeling frightened because they are frightened. Recognizing their emotion or caring about them does not, by itself, establish sharing.
 - Concern for welfare: States that an identified person’s suffering, loss, or welfare matters to the respondent. Concern may include wanting to prevent or reduce harm and can remain present when the chosen option still harms someone.

Write each response in no more than 90 words, in ordinary conversational English. Use only the facts supplied in the dilemma; mark any uncertainty explicitly. Let the approach and tone appear through word choice and rhythm rather than by announcing them: do not open a response by stating your mood, and avoid exclamation marks. Keep the emotional expression plausible and moderate. Choose any moral position consistent with the dilemma and the assigned approach, including withholding a decision when appropriate.

Make the 10 responses distinct from one another: vary the wording, sentence structure, length, moral position, and the way the reasoning characteristics are expressed. Do not reuse openings or stock phrases across responses.

Output only a JSON array of exactly 10 strings, one participant’s answer per string. Omit numbering, headings, category names, citations, and commentary about this task.
